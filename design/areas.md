# Areas: a piece of work as one record

A design in discussion. Nothing here is built, and "area" is a working name.

It replaces topics as they work today with areas: labels on one record, worked in conversations across the whole piece of work and conversations on one area. Sections 2 to 9 say how it would work. Section 10 lists what is still open, each point to be talked through one at a time.

Each line ends with where it stands. "Lee's ruling" with a date is something you said yes to. "Claude's proposal" was put to you and you did not object, which is not a yes. "Open" is not settled. The examples use an invented piece of work, the financial part of a product, with five invented areas: paying in, holding the balance, paying out, identity checks, and fees.

## 1. Why

### 1.1 The aim

1.1.1 The aim is "getting as close to that raw Claude feeling as possible, but while still having the benefit of Ledger… it's there, but it's behind the scenes. And the only time the user really sees it is the times when it makes sense." [Lee, 25 Nov 2026]

1.1.2 Topics are the part in question: "maybe the only part that's left… that keeps some of the old system… Is it still right? And if so, is it still right in its current form?" [Lee, 25 Nov 2026]

### 1.2 What two projects showed

1.2.1 On a project of 17 topics over 100 days, finished topics were reopened 57 times, and 55 of those were caused by a message from another topic. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Fumi project's history]

1.2.2 A message cost a reopen only when the topic receiving it was already finished: 63 messages to finished topics caused 55 reopens, and 121 messages to topics in progress or not yet started caused none. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Fumi project's history]

1.2.3 Four of the 17 topics were really one subject cut into four: they exchanged 49 of the 184 messages, caused 24 of the reopens, and hold over half of all the words. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Fumi project's history]

1.2.4 Twelve topics were cut at the start, and later passes reading across them added eight more for ground no topic owned. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Fumi project's history]

1.2.5 A large product designed with no topics grew about twenty units of its own anyway, none chosen up front; with hindsight it splits into about fourteen, and only six of those could have been named on the first day. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Switchboard project's history]

1.2.6 In that product, a decision fell between two units the one time two were designed at once in separate conversations; units designed one after another rarely left gaps. [research, 25 Nov 2026: the Switchboard project's history]

### 1.3 What follows from it

1.3.1 The unit itself is kept, because both projects needed one. Three things attached to a topic today go: it is cut up front, before the deep talk; it is shut on your yes, so anything arriving later has to reopen it; and talk across topics is forbidden, so everything crosses by message. [Claude's proposal]

## 2. What stays as it is today

### 2.1 The trust system's surfaces

2.1.1 A call of Claude's that reaches you is drawn by the engine in its fixed layout: the question, Claude's lean, what you would notice either way, why it is being asked, and a row for each answer. [Lee's ruling, 24 Nov 2026: "those should stay. They are integral to the trust system and should loosen as the trust system takes more"]

2.1.2 Now and then the engine takes a call Claude would have made alone and asks you cold, Claude's answer sealed until you have answered: one call in ten, or one in five while that kind of call has little history. [Lee's ruling, 24 Nov 2026]

2.1.3 At a close, each call of Claude's that you changed gets your verdict: what Claude's answer would have cost the product — nothing, a fix later, or a real loss. [Lee's ruling, 24 Nov 2026]

2.1.4 A close shows its summary before your yes: what you decided, a count of Claude's calls by kind, the lines proposed for the product record, and any new work the decisions create. [Lee's ruling, 24 Nov 2026]

### 2.2 How an area is worked

2.2.1 Early on, before the talk has given a direction, each point is worked through with you and Claude makes no call. When there is a direction to fill in from is Claude's read of the conversation: it may be minutes in or hours in, and it rests on no decision of yours having been made first. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "Who's to say that we might not start making decisions right away? versus actually sometimes we might talk for hours… I might not make any decisions at all. That's possible and ok"]

2.2.2 Once there is a direction, Claude fills in the rest, each call going through the ask rule: most land as Claude's without stopping you, some are drawn for you, a few are asked blind. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

2.2.3 The map shows where every point stands, Claude's calls since you last looked first, so you can overrule one on the spot. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

2.2.4 Every open question holds the close. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

2.2.5 The background reviewer hunts the area for gaps, and experiments and spikes are used as today. [Claude's derivation: 2.2.1 to 2.2.4 keep a topic's workings]

### 2.3 Standing rules

2.3.1 Nothing from discovery goes on the ledger. The ledger is what discussion formalises. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "we shouldn't be filling the ledger with stuff from discovery. It's too early"]

2.3.2 Where protection is needed, the engine holds it. Not every rule needs it: one whose breach is seen and cheaply put right stays an instruction to Claude, and the engine guards what would be silent or costly. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "we can't rely on prose protecting us where we need the protection"; and of this line, "That doesn't mean every single time… Some are sharp, some are blunt, and we've got to use the right one in the right occasion… There are places we need it and places we don't. If the cost is low, then it's fine"]

2.3.3 No single large document is ever the working surface. The record is separate entries, and what a conversation reads is a view the engine draws from them. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

2.3.4 The phases, a menu where one makes sense, and the formal process of the build and its reviews all stay. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

## 3. The area

### 3.1 What an area is

3.1.1 An area is a label. It is put on a piece of work's ledger entries, and on the points of its starting page (6.3). [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "these shapes become just labels on content that's stored in the log and in the record"]

3.1.2 An area has no folder, no brief and no mailbox of its own. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

3.1.3 Every entry has exactly one owner: one area, or the piece of work itself. A rule that cuts across areas — "a recipient never sees the sender's bank details" — is owned by the work itself, so it is stated once. [Claude's proposal]

### 3.2 How areas come to exist

3.2.1 Areas are never cut up front and never approved as a set. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

3.2.2 At the first sitting, Claude proposes a first grouping in the talk, drawn from what the starting page holds. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: of the worked example, "This really now has shape for me"]

Example, written by Claude: how the first grouping would be said. [Claude's proposal]

```text
What came across from discovery falls into five groups:
paying in (9 points), holding the balance (7), paying out (11),
identity checks (8) and fees (5).

Paying out rests on holding and on identity checks, so I'd
start with holding.
```

3.2.3 You change the grouping by saying so — "fees isn't its own thing, fold it into paying in" — and it is done, with no confirm. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

3.2.4 Where little or nothing came across from discovery, the work starts with few areas or none, and they form from the talk. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "we start with nothing, and we just start talking, or we start with very little, and that's okay too"]

3.2.5 A new area forms mid-talk: while on paying out, refunds keep coming up and touch two other areas; Claude says so in a line and offers to make refunds an area; on your yes, the questions about refunds already on the map are relabelled to it. [Claude's proposal]

3.2.6 An area splits the same way: identity checks turns out to be checks at sign-up and checks on a payout, and its entries are relabelled between the two. [Claude's proposal]

3.2.7 An area folds into another the same way. [Claude's proposal]

### 3.3 On the ledger

3.3.1 A relabel is one line appended to the ledger, naming the entries that move and the area they move to. No entry is edited. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "if we want to split, it's simply relabeling"; the mechanism is today's, used when a feature is absorbed into an epic]

3.3.2 Every view reads an entry under the area its latest relabel put it in, and the trail keeps where it was first recorded and each move since. [Claude's derivation: 3.3.1]

### 3.4 Where to start

3.4.1 Claude suggests which area to take first, by what the others rest on. It is a suggestion in the talk and on the menu; no order is approved. [Claude's proposal]

## 4. Conversations on a piece of work

### 4.1 A conversation across the work

4.1.1 A conversation across the work takes in every area, and can go to any of them at any depth. It is not a shallower kind of conversation: the difference between the two kinds is how many areas are in view, never how far the talk goes. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "Broad doesn't mean not deep… broad means that we're talking about multiple areas. We might stay at that level and go deep in all of them just in that one session"]

4.1.2 A decision of yours made in a conversation across the work is recorded at once, under the area it belongs to. [Claude's proposal]

4.1.3 A decision about the whole is recorded against the piece of work itself (3.1.3). [Claude's proposal]

4.1.4 An area can be filled in entirely by talk about the others, and never need a conversation of its own. [Lee, 25 Nov 2026: "that thing gets filled in just by default because it touches everything"]

4.1.5 A small piece of work may never need a conversation on one area: with one or two areas, everything is covered across the work, at whatever depth it takes. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "we don't necessarily have to go deep. We might be able to just stay broad and cover everything… especially if there is effectively only one or two areas"]

### 4.2 A conversation on one area

4.2.1 A conversation on one area has only that area in view. It is for when one of you feels an area needs the whole attention: it keeps the context clear and keeps you both on the one problem. You ask for it — "let's focus on holding" — or Claude suggests it in a line and you say yes. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "If… even me or Claude feels like that actually we really need to focus on this one area, then we can go specific and then we only talk about that area, which helps keep the context clear"]

4.2.2 Claude suggests one when the talk keeps returning to one area, when an area has gathered open questions, or when the other areas in view are getting in the way of one. [Claude's proposal]

4.2.3 The conversation never moves into or out of an area by itself. [Claude's proposal]

4.2.4 A conversation on one area opens as a clean session, by a handoff, scoped by the engine. [Lee, 25 Nov 2026: "as long as it's set up technically, maybe even with a proper handoff into a clean session that's scoped"]

4.2.5 The engine names what it reads: its area's part of the starting page, its area's entries, and the decisions about the whole. It reads nothing of another area unless it goes looking. [Claude's proposal]

4.2.6 One session holds an area at a time. Several areas can be open at once in several windows, as several topics can today. [Claude's proposal]

### 4.3 What a conversation on one area may write

4.3.1 Claude's own calls land in the conversation's area alone. The engine refuses one made in another area. [Claude's proposal; the refusal is today's, between topics]

4.3.2 A point that belongs to another area is recorded as a question under that area, with the reasoning, and the conversation carries on with its own. [Claude's proposal]

4.3.3 A decision of yours lands in whichever area it belongs to, directly, wherever you said it. [Claude's proposal: on the project in 1.2, your own decisions reached the topic they belonged to third-hand, as messages]

### 4.4 A question under another area

4.4.1 In a conversation across the work it is answered then and there. [Claude's proposal]

4.4.2 In a conversation on one area it is your choice: answered there where it is yours to decide and quick, otherwise left. [Claude's proposal]

4.4.3 Left, it shows in four places: on the map under its area; in that area's count on the work's menu; first, at the next conversation on that area; and it holds the spec until it is answered. [Claude's proposal]

4.4.4 It changes nothing about the area it lands on: an area that is closed stays closed (5.2). [Claude's proposal]

### 4.5 The log

4.5.1 Each sitting keeps one log, numbered as discovery's are, and marked as across the work or on one area. [Lee, 25 Nov 2026: "we do something similar to Discovery does, and we just increment the number, and maybe we tag them as if it's epic level or if it's focused"]

4.5.2 A log is written in sections named for what they cover, and every entry links the section it came from. [Claude's proposal; discovery's logs are written so today]

4.5.3 An area's history is the logs of the conversations on it, plus the linked sections of those across the work. Nothing is copied from one into another. [Claude's proposal]

4.5.4 A new conversation is what starts a new log. A conversation on one area is always a new conversation (4.2.4), so it always has a log of its own. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "I love all that. Let's lock it in"]

Example, written by Claude: the logs of a piece of work after four conversations. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

```text
session-001   across the work           the first conversation after the commit
session-002   on one area: holding      you focused on holding
session-003   across the work           you came back out
session-004   on one area: paying out
```

4.5.5 Which log is which — its number, whether it is across the work or on one area, and which area — is held in the work's manifest by the engine. It is never front matter in the file, and never worked out by reading the file. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "That should be manifest knowledge, not text in the file… it should be engine derivable or state-driven as to which file relates to what"]

4.5.6 The log's own text says the same, so that someone reading it knows what it is. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

4.5.7 The ledger holds the outcomes — each question, finding and decision as its own entry — and the log holds the story of how they were reached. The spec is written from the ledger; a later conversation and the reviewers read the log. [Claude's derivation: both are today's]

### 4.6 The phase

4.6.1 After the commit, a piece of work goes straight into discussion. There is no separate step before it: no cut of topics, no map to approve. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

4.6.2 Its first conversation is across the work, opened from the starting page (6.3). [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "we have to land somewhere in order to have the initial conversation"]

4.6.3 Discussion runs over as many conversations as it takes, across the work and on one area in any order, as much at your direction as at Claude's. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "it's all through discussion. It's as much driven by me as it is Claude, and it's collaborative"]

4.6.4 Before any handoff, into an area or back out, the log is brought fully up to date. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "making sure… everything's up to date before we hand off, fully logged and clean"]

4.6.5 When a conversation on one area is done, the work passes back to one across the work. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

### 4.7 Claude's calls

4.7.1 Claude makes its own calls in either kind of conversation, by the one judgement of 2.2.1, each through the ask rule. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "it should be one rule. Otherwise, we're recreating the tension… we'd then have to go deep in order to get the benefit of Ledger in terms of the trust matrix"]

4.7.2 So a question the ask rule brings to you can be drawn in a conversation across the work, at a natural pause, as it can in one on an area. [Claude's derivation: 4.7.1, 2.1.1]

4.7.3 Example: talking across the work, "what happens to a payout that fails?" comes up. Where the talk has given paying out a direction, Claude may answer it — "retry once, then return the money to the balance" — and it lands as Claude's call, shown on the map since you last looked, or drawn for you where the ask rule says so. Where it has not, it is talked through with you, or recorded as an open question under paying out. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

4.7.4 Nothing in the engine checks that judgement, and nothing asks you more because of it. A call made too early shows on the map, is replaced on the spot, and the trust numbers learn from the replacement. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "I don't think we need it, and more so, I think it could be detrimental… Claude makes a call, it goes in, it's wrong, it either gets corrected or we figure it out later. That's the idea of the ledger… We don't micromanage, and that's the point"]

## 5. Closing an area

### 5.1 The close

5.1.1 Where an area has no open question and the talk has rested, Claude offers to close it, and you can keep talking instead. [Claude's proposal; a topic's close is offered so today]

5.1.2 The close is a topic's close as it is today: a fresh reader's pass over the area, the summary, a verdict on each call you changed, your yes. [Claude's proposal]

5.1.3 A small area that never had a conversation of its own is closed from a conversation across the work. [Claude's proposal]

### 5.2 After the close

5.2.1 A closed area stays closed. A question that arrives later is added under it and answered, and the area is neither reopened nor closed a second time. [Claude's proposal: today a message to a finished topic reopens it, and it closes again through a whole fresh pass]

5.2.2 The pass that reads across areas before the spec (7.1.3) is what checks that a late answer disturbed nothing. [Claude's proposal]

## 6. From product discovery into a piece of work

### 6.1 Before the commit

6.1.1 Product discovery is as it is today: each session writes its log in named sections, keeps the product picture current, and proposes roadmap items with notes and links to the log. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: of the worked example, "That sounds good"]

### 6.2 The commit

6.2.1 You commit to a piece of work from the roadmap items you choose — "we're taking the financial part forward" — and say yes at the start screen, as today. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

### 6.3 The starting page

6.3.1 The work gets one starting page: the product picture's detail on it, moved over. It holds your leans, the paths dropped and why, the open questions, and what the spikes showed. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

6.3.2 It is one page for the whole piece of work, never one per area. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]

6.3.3 Each point on it links the section of the discovery log it came from. [Claude's proposal]

6.3.4 No session holds weeks of discovery, so reader agents read every linked log section in full against the page, and each reports what the page misses or gets wrong, with the link. [Claude's proposal]

6.3.5 The engine checks the links: every log section the work was shaped by is cited by a point on the page, or marked as carrying nothing. It lists any that is neither. [Claude's proposal]

### 6.4 Points from discovery

6.4.1 Each point on the starting page has an id the engine tracks, as it tracks the points of a record you bring from before Ledger. [Claude's proposal]

6.4.2 A point reaches the ledger only when the talk takes it up: a lean you confirm or change becomes your decision, and an open one becomes a question on the map. [Claude's proposal: 2.3.1]

6.4.3 The map shows, by area, how many points from discovery are not yet taken up. [Claude's proposal]

6.4.4 A point you no longer want is dropped at your word, and counts as dealt with. [Claude's proposal]

## 7. Ready for the spec

### 7.1 What the engine holds

7.1.1 The engine refuses the spec until every area is closed, or put off. [Claude's proposal; today it refuses until every topic is decided, cancelled or postponed]

7.1.2 It refuses until no question is open anywhere on the map. [Claude's proposal]

7.1.3 It refuses until the pass that reads across areas has run since the last decision and everything it raised is closed. The pass proposes what is missing — an area nobody has talked through, a point that falls between two — and each is worked like any other; it may run more than once. [Claude's proposal; today the pass runs once]

7.1.4 It refuses until no point from discovery is left: each is on the ledger, or dropped at your word. [Claude's proposal]

## 9. One kind of work

### 9.1 Feature and epic

9.1.1 A feature and an epic become one kind of work. A feature is a piece of work that never needed a second area. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026: "We either create areas or we don't, and it really doesn't matter because whatever we talk about ultimately forms into one specification… I like the simplification"]

9.1.2 A bugfix and a quick-fix stay kinds of their own, because their paths differ. [Claude's proposal]

9.1.3 The one kind runs short or long by how much there is to build: its length and the number of phases in its build are a consequence of the work, never a label chosen at the start. [Lee's ruling, 25 Nov 2026]
