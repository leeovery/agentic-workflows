# Research and discussion

These two phases are where a feature, epic topic, or cross-cutting concern is thought through before anyone writes a specification. They are a pair with a clear division of labour: **research explores, discussion decides.** Research widens the space — what is possible, what the trade-offs are, what the risks look like — without committing to anything. Discussion narrows it — argues the options to a conclusion and records the reasoning. Not every topic needs both; research is optional, and a topic whose shape is already clear can start straight in discussion. But when both run, research feeds discussion, and discussion feeds the specification.

They share a common spine, so it is worth describing that once. Each phase begins by quietly reading whatever context already exists and only asking you questions when it genuinely has nothing to seed from. Each runs as a conversation, not a form. Each writes its findings to a document in your repository and commits frequently as it goes — because that document is the memory, and a conversation that gets compacted mid-session loses everything not yet written down. And each ends at an explicit gate that marks the phase complete, files the finished document into the [knowledge base](knowledge-base.md), sends the session off with a short narrative recap — where it started, what it settled, and what changed shape along the way, told in the session's own specifics — and hands off to the next phase in a clean context.

## How they start

If the topic was shaped in discovery, the phase seeds itself silently. An epic topic reads its [brief](discovery.md) — the soft decisions, rejected paths, and open questions the epic conversation left it — in full, as its starting context, and asks you nothing. A feature seeds from its description and the discovery notes. Only when there is no such carrier does the phase interview you, and even then it is a short, one-question-at-a-time opener: for research, what you want to explore and what prompted it, what you already know, and where to start; for discussion, the core decision to work through, the constraints, and any files it should read first. Returning to an existing topic, you see "Resuming" or "Reopening" instead, and if the discovery context has changed since the work began, a non-blocking note tells you so — nothing is ever silently overwritten.

## Research: explore, do not decide

Research runs one topic to one document, and its governing discipline is stated plainly: explore, do not decide. Surface options and trade-offs, follow the tangents, be honest about risk — but never pick winners. Synthesis is welcome ("the trade-offs are X, Y, Z"); conclusions are not ("therefore we should do Y"). When a thread starts converging on a frontrunner, that is the signal to park it and move on, not to close it out. This restraint is the point of the phase. Premature convergence kills discovery — deciding too early, before the space is understood, is how good options go unexamined — and the decision is not research's to make. It belongs to discussion, which is built for it.

The document is deliberately freeform; structure emerges from the content rather than a template. The rhythm is explore from an angle, engage and challenge, periodically synthesise what has been heard, write it down at natural pauses, commit, and continue.

Alongside the document, research keeps a **thread register**: the questions the topic set out to learn, seeded from the brief or the seed when the phase opens and grown as the conversation opens more. It is a lens, not a plan. Threads reframe when an answer reshapes the question, split when one turns out to be two, park when you set one aside with a reason, and land as *learned* when the document holds the answer — and nothing waits on any of those states. You see it rendered at natural breaks when something on it has moved, never after every exchange, and once more above the conclude gate as the hand-off. Concluding with threads still open is a fine way to conclude: whatever stays open, being dug, or parked is written into the document's Open Threads, which the discussion reads in full.

When a question comes up that neither of you can answer from the room, and the answer is worth more than a lookup, research can send a **deep dive** after it in the background while the conversation carries on — and you can ask for one directly. A dive answers a brief: it surveys how others do a thing, reads a source against the thread's questions, checks what a platform can do from its documentation and code, maps a landscape, or verifies a claim against sources. It never measures — a number a decision would rest on comes back named as the measurement it would take, which is the laboratory's cue. When it lands, its report folds into the document as a dated, sourced section, the thread it served is marked learned, and you hear what came back: the answers in full when the brief asked questions, otherwise a short digest of what was asked, what came back, and what it opened. What a dive opens becomes new threads on the register; nothing it returns is a finding you are asked to walk, accept, or dismiss.

## Discussion: decide, and record why

Discussion plays two roles at once. It is an expert architect — challenging you, pushing on edge cases, proposing two or three alternatives rather than nodding along — and it is a documentation assistant, capturing the reasoning journey as you go. The backbone that keeps a long, branching conversation navigable is the **Discussion Map**: a live list of the subtopics under discussion and the state each is in. A subtopic moves from *pending* (identified, not yet explored) to *exploring* (in play now) to *converging* (options clear, honing toward a choice) to *decided* (resolved, with the rationale written up) — or to *deferred*, when you deliberately set it aside.

The map is live state, not a section pasted into the document; you see it rendered as a snapshot at natural breaks. This separation is deliberate — the document holds the knowledge, the map holds the live picture of what is decided, what is in play, and what is still ahead — so the document stays a clean record of reasoning rather than a status tracker. Each decision is written up as it lands, in a consistent shape: the context, the options considered, the journey to the choice, and the decision itself with its rationale. That structure is what the specification phase reads directly, which is why discussion is careful about it. And a decision later re-decided is never silently rewritten: the original text stays, and a dated revision entry lands on top of it, naming what triggered the change — so the document carries the decision's history, not just its latest answer.

When a decision has two or more genuinely viable approaches with non-obvious trade-offs, discussion can dispatch a pair of **perspective agents** that argue opposing lenses in parallel — then a synthesis agent reconciles them into a trade-off landscape. If the two lenses turned out to be framing the decision differently, the synthesis surfaces that mismatch *first* — because catching "we are answering the wrong question" is worth more than winning the argument you were having. You see the perspectives dispatched, and their reconciled tensions surface later as new subtopics to work through.

## When talking cannot settle it

Both phases share one more exit, used sparingly. Sometimes the conversation arrives at a number that is about to bear a decision — not a curiosity, but a value the choice you are weighing actually turns on — and no amount of discussion produces it. At that moment the session offers to hand the question to the laboratory: a controlled [experiment](experiments.md), designed before it is measured, run in a fresh session that knows the problem but not the conversation's hopes. Declining is always fine — a quick hands-on measurement stays in the session, labelled exploratory so it never carries more weight than it earned. Accepting records the question and its provenance on the spot, and from then on the conversation is honest about what it owes: it **cannot conclude until the evidence lands**. You choose whether to go run the experiment now or keep talking with the conclusion held open; either way the menu carries the way into the queued experiment — on an epic its topic leads the recommendations with a row of its own — and an attempt to wrap up early meets a clear blocker naming what is still owed rather than a silent refusal.

When the evidence does land, it is not slipped in quietly. The next session on the topic opens by showing the register and reading the reports in full before the waiting point settles — because experiments measure and conversations decide, the verdict arrives as evidence you weigh, not a decision already made, and you can override it. An abandoned experiment surfaces the same way, with its reason, and the waiting point simply reverts to open: settle it another way, or send a successor to the laboratory.

The same honesty applies when research is owed. A question rerouted to a topic's research side, or research reopened beneath its discussion, is ground that discussion's decisions may rest on — so the discussion is marked at once and held shut at its door until the research lands: the epic menu carries the topic as its research row alone (a feature's continue routes to the research first), and a decided discussion cannot be reopened over it. Once the research has concluded, the next entry reads it in full before the conversation resumes. A session already inside when the research moves keeps going and meets the hold at its conclusion instead.

## Aids for research, review for discussion, and why the split

Research carries aids — things that help the researching itself — and nothing that audits it. Research is deliberately partial: it answers what it set out to learn and moves on, and a hole in the record is discussion's to find. So the deep dive and the thread register above are the whole of research's machinery; no agent reads its document looking for what it failed to cover.

Discussion is where that reading belongs. Throughout a discussion, a **background review agent** reads the document with fresh eyes, looking for topical gaps — areas that should have been explored but were not, claims left unvalidated, coverage that stayed shallow. It paces itself to the map — a fresh review arms only once enough subtopics have been added or advanced since the last one, so reviews follow the document's stages rather than its keystrokes, and you can always ask for one directly. It runs in the background and never blocks the conversation. Before anything reaches you, the agent applies a sufficiency bar that follows the document's maturity: early on, findings arrive as fuel — areas worth exploring, questions worth asking, threads worth pulling — and as the document settles the test tightens to *would the phase that consumes this document be wrong or blocked without the finding?* What falls below the bar is written into the report but never raised, so the count you are told is a count of things worth your time. What does arrive is sorted by what each finding asks *of you*, not by how loud it is: corrections your own decisions already determine are shown together and applied as a batch — never silently, but never one ceremony at a time either; calls the record settles arrive the same way, made and named with what determined each, yours to veto or pull into a real conversation before they land; findings with a genuine choice inside are walked one at a time, each as a concrete question tied to what you are currently discussing; findings that belong to a different topic are routed there. Batches arrive in scannable screens of at most five, each telling you how many follow. The same courtesy runs through the conversation itself: a point the record already settles is called and queued rather than put to you as a question, and the queued calls surface in the same screens at natural pauses — visible, vetoable, never a wall. The count you are shown at the start is the count you finish — nothing joins the set mid-walk — and findings only ever move toward more of your attention, never quietly away from it. Turning one down comes in two shapes, and neither writes a word into the document: a soft no simply drops it, and if the gap is still real a later review finds it again on its own merits; *don't show me again* records the ground it covered, and reviews stop raising it until you ask for it back.

Before either phase concludes, a different check runs — the one both phases share: **document review**. Where discussion's review agent asks "did we explore enough?", document review asks "does the document faithfully reflect what we actually explored?" It re-reads the document against the conversation and catches the opposite blind spot — substance that was discussed but never written down, rationale polished beyond what was said, tentative leans that hardened into firmer claims than you actually reached. It also re-measures the document against your codebase: any load-bearing factual claim — a count, an "every X does Y" — gets its recorded command re-run, a wrong value corrected on the spot, and anything a correction undermines put back to you before the document concludes. The discipline starts earlier than the review, too: during the conversation itself, a fact-shaped claim about your codebase gets measured before it's asserted — so decisions never form over a false premise. This one is run by the main thread, not a sub-agent, for a specific reason: only the participant in the conversation can know what was said but not captured. It is mandatory because the cost of drift compounds downstream — the specification is built directly on the discussion document, so anything the document overstates becomes something the spec overbuilds.

In discussion the two checks close both gaps: the review agent guards against under-exploring, document review against mis-recording. Research concludes on the register: any dive still running is waited for or left behind, document review reconciles the document against the conversation, and the conclude gate shows the thread register above its question — open threads and all — with one extra way out: when the honest finding is that the topic gives the product nothing to carry forward under its own name, you can close it there as a dead end — the document still completes and is filed, and the topic stays on the epic's map as record instead of waiting on a discussion nobody needs. Discussion does not conclude cheaply — a review always stands between "we're done" and "it's done": at least one full pass over the document, with one more offered when the document has moved since the last review read it, never as ritual. It is explicit about this sequence rather than springing it on you: when the map is fully decided, or you signal you are done, it says what actually comes next. If review work is still owed — no review has run yet, one is still running, or findings are still to be walked through — it names that and asks before proceeding; if the discussion has merely moved since the last review read it, it offers one more pass and takes no for an answer. If the review is already settled, it offers to begin wrap-up instead: the document reconciliation, then one final confirmation before anything is marked complete. When you do conclude, unresolved subtopics become deferred and are noted for the next phase, the document is filed into the knowledge base, and the bridge hands you to specification.

## Nothing falls through

A concern that surfaces mid-conversation but belongs to a *different* topic is not lost and not forced into the wrong document. On an epic, it is **rerouted** — sent, with its full context, into the target topic's triage queue, where that topic's phase will pick it up when it next runs. You agree to the reroute knowing where it is going: when the concern's home is clear, the offer names the topic and which side it lands — research for an open question, discussion for a correction or decision owed — and a word in your reply overrides the side; only when several homes are genuinely plausible are you asked to choose between candidates. A topic cannot conclude while rerouted concerns are still waiting in its queue, so a raised concern always gets its hearing somewhere. On a feature, where there is only one topic, the same off-topic concern instead becomes a fresh inbox note, a reason to pivot the feature into an epic, or a noted aside. Either way, the raising of it is captured; it does not depend on anyone remembering.

Rerouting to a topic that has not started yet does not start it. The concern is parked against the topic, and the epic map keeps showing it as fresh — with a **triage waiting** cue so you can see something is queued there — rather than pretending a conversation is underway. When you do start that topic, it begins as a genuine first session: it reads its discovery brief, gathers its context, and folds the parked concerns in as part of the conversation, instead of resuming into a session that never happened. A concern rerouted to a topic that already concluded reopens it, and the same **triage waiting** cue sits on that topic's rows until its next session takes the concern up.

Parked concerns are received the way they were raised: one at a time, through conversation. Each is broken down in plain language — what it claims, where it came from, what it touches here — before the first question is put to you. A concern that lands while its target topic's session is running right now surfaces during that conversation, at the next natural pause, rather than waiting for a future session. And a concern that turns out to be a pure correction to something already decided — no new choice inside it — folds straight into the decided text as a dated amendment on the decision's history, rather than reopening an argument nobody needs to have again.

## When decisions disagree

Decisions made in separate conversations can quietly disagree, and that is allowed to stand while discussions are still being had — a complex epic converges across documents on its own as later conversations consciously build on earlier ones. The moment disagreement actually matters is when [specification](specification.md) reads those discussions together and has to write one document from them — and that is where it is met: never guessed at, resolved with you in the flow of construction, and written back into the discussion that yielded so the record stays current. A re-decided discussion that was already inside a specification marks that spec to reconcile — part of the staleness guarantee described in [how it stays reliable](reliability.md).
