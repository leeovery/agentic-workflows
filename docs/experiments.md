# Experiments

Some questions cannot be argued to an answer. Mid-way through a [research or discussion](research-and-discussion.md) session, the conversation sometimes arrives at a number that is about to bear a decision — how often does the sandbox really deliver a webhook twice, what does that index actually cost at this catalogue size — and no amount of talking settles it. Experiments are the laboratory those conversations hand such questions to: a controlled measurement, designed before it is run, producing a result dependable enough to decide on. The governing rule is stated once and everything else follows from it: **the design exists before the data**. A number collected before its question, prediction, and decision rule were written down is a sighting, not a result — it can motivate an experiment, never settle one.

## The offer

You are never forced into the laboratory. Conversations still poke at things hands-on, and that stays legitimate — those sightings are simply labelled exploratory in the document, so they never carry weight they were not built for. The offer comes only when a measurement is about to carry decision-bearing weight, and it comes as a plain question in the conversation's own voice: here is what a controlled experiment would settle, and here is what a dependable answer takes off the table. Declining is always valid — a quick inline measurement, no ceremony, remains part of the conversation. Accepting graduates the question.

On yes, the spawn is recorded right there, while the conversation still holds the knowledge. The experiment gets an id in the topic's series — E1, E2, and so on — and a problem statement written in plain terms: what needs picking or learning, the space around it, what the conversation hopes, and where the question was born. Deliberately, **nothing more**. The spawning conversation is the client at the laboratory door: it states the problem and stops. Refining the question, predicting the outcome, writing the decision rule, choosing the method — all of that belongs to the laboratory, precisely so the measurement is not shaped by the conversation's hopes.

Two things then happen at once. The conversation is **locked**: it cannot conclude until the experiment's evidence lands, because it has raised a question it needs answered before it can honestly finish — trying to wrap up early meets a clear blocker naming the open experiments, with the choice to pause or keep talking. And you choose when to go: work the experiment now — the session pauses where it stands and the menu takes over — or later, carrying the conversation on with the conclusion held open. Either way the menu carries the way into every queued experiment — on an epic's dashboard each live one leads the recommendations with an entry of its own, retiring when its record ends — and the pick stays yours.

## The laboratory

An experiment always begins in a fresh session, and this is a scientific control rather than a convenience. A measurement run inside the conversation that spawned it would know what the conversation hopes for, and that biases the result. So the laboratory starts cold, reads the problem statement and the spawning conversation's document from disk, and then designs — collaboratively, asking you its own questions.

The design is where your involvement is front-loaded. Together you settle the question and the decision it feeds, the prediction and why, the rule that will settle it — written concretely enough that a third party could execute it against the results without judgment ("if X, we do A; if Y, B") — and the setup: method, instruments, sample, environment. One experiment answers one primary question; anything else it measures is explicitly secondary. Depth scales with the shape — a ten-minute local test writes four lines per section, a multi-day run writes pages — but the skeleton never scales away.

Then comes the **briefing**: the design retold in plain terms — what we'll do, what we expect and why, what each outcome triggers — and your confirmation freezes it. The freeze is the whole discipline in one moment. From here until the first result is visible, the design changes only through dated amendments you re-confirm; once results are visible it is frozen for good, because a rule changed after seeing data voids the run, and old data is never re-scored under new rules. A flaw found late goes into the report's corrections — dated, append-only — and triggers the *next* experiment rather than quietly rewriting this one.

The run itself is mostly autonomous — that is what the front-loaded collaboration bought. The session executes the setup as designed, choosing whatever execution shape produces the most dependable results — doing it directly, writing deterministic code and observing it run, background processes, helper agents for independent legs — and a run can be minutes or days; the scope is narrow, the work inside it is not. Results land in the report as they are measured, every number traceable to a file in the experiment's own records or a named source, and deviations — the harness broke, the environment surprised — are logged as they happen, so the report shows the run as it went rather than a reconstruction.

Occasionally a running experiment discovers its question was really two. That split is the laboratory's internal method, invisible to the conversation waiting on it: the parts become sub-experiments — E1.1, E1.2 — each walking design, freeze, run, and report in miniature, and E1's verdict synthesises them. The lock stays on E1 and releases once, when E1 as a whole ends.

## The verdict, and who decides

An experiment concludes by executing its pre-registered decision rule against the results — which branch fired and what it triggers — and recording a one-line verdict. Reading is kept separate from measurement in the report, and a measure conceived after seeing the data is labelled exploratory: it can motivate a successor, never settle this run.

The verdict is deliberately mechanical, and that is because **experiments measure; conversations decide.** When the lock releases, the paused conversation becomes the recommendation again, and its next session opens on the evidence: the topic's register of experiments, and the reports read in full, before the waiting point settles. The conversation weighs the verdict as evidence and can override it — the rule's outcome is an input to your decision, not a decision made for you. The evidence reaches the eventual [specification](specification.md) only through the discussion that weighed it; a spec never reads an experiment directly.

An experiment can also be **abandoned** at any point before it concludes — the question went moot, or the world moved on — with its reason recorded. Abandonment is a first-class ending, not an erasure: the row and its reason stay on the register, the lock releases, and the waiting point reverts to open when the conversation next surfaces it — you settle it another way, or spawn a successor.

## What is left behind

A topic accumulates a series — every experiment its conversations ever spawned — and the **register** is its full measurement history: one row per experiment, conceived to verdict, abandoned rows kept with their reasons. Each record's documents live in your repository with the rest of the work: the problem statement, the frozen design, the growing report, the data extracts the report cites, and any harness code the run built — instrument, not product code, kept with the record so the result can be re-run and re-checked.

One deliberate absence: experiments never enter the [knowledge base](knowledge-base.md). Their results take whatever shape the problem demands — tables, images, logs, any mix — and they have exactly one consumer: the conversation that asked, which digests them into its own document. *That* document is what the system remembers.
