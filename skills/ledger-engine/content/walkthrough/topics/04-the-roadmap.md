# The roadmap

Some conversations are about the product rather than a piece of work: a new product being conceived, what the next few months should hold, a v2 taking shape. Those happen in the product's own conversation, the start menu's `p` row, and what they shape lands on the roadmap. It sits above the pieces of work and holds everything decided on but not yet started: ideas, bugs and quick-fixes alike.

Items are what it holds, each marked as an idea, a bug or a quick-fix, with a one-line summary and a pointer back to where it came from. Horizons are the buckets they sit in, named in your own staging words, and their order is the meaning: the first horizon is what comes next. A horizon you call Backlog or Later is your backlog; the roadmap has no other.

```
   ── launch ──────────────────────────────────────
        items you need before anything ships
   ── v1 ──────────────────────────────────────────
        items for the release after that
   ── someday ─────────────────────────────────────
        items you want on record but not yet
```

Things land on it in several ways. Ask in the product's conversation and Claude lays out the items and horizons it heard. Mid-way through any other conversation, say where something belongs ("that's a v2 thing") and it goes straight onto that horizon while the conversation carries on. A topic of an epic that belongs to a later release is postponed onto it rather than cancelled, and comes back when it is pulled forward into an epic whose map is still open: its own, where it returns as it was, or another, where it opens as a fresh topic that reads the earlier record and never changes it. And inbox items you decide to do, but not now, go on from triage. The horizon is always yours: where you haven't named one, Claude asks, and never picks one itself.

Work begins when you pick items on the roadmap's `w` row. They go into discovery together as the seeds of one piece of work, and discovery settles its kind, a quick-fix included. Whatever you didn't pick stays visibly waiting. A started item leaves the roadmap, which shows only what still waits; if its work is cancelled, the item returns to waiting.
