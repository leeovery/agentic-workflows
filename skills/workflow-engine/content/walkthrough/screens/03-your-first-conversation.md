# Your first conversation

Everything starts the same way: you press `s` and describe what's on your mind, in whatever words come naturally. You don't need to know what kind of work it is, or whether it's a piece of work at all yet. The system's first job is to understand the shape of what you're telling it. Sometimes you're describing the product as a whole, with no single piece of work on the table, and the conversation simply carries on at that level. More often it's one thing, and the question becomes what kind of thing: something that used to work and now doesn't, something small and mechanical with nothing to debate, a standard for the codebase to follow rather than something to ship, one coherent piece of work, or several distinct things travelling under one name.

It gets there by asking one question at a time and telling you what it's hearing as it goes, so a wrong read can be corrected the moment it appears. When the picture holds steady it says what it thinks this is and why, and asks whether it has read you right. Until you say yes, nothing has been written anywhere, and walking away costs you nothing.

```
           you describe what's on your mind
                          ↓
      about the whole product?      ──→  the roadmap
                          ↓  no, one piece of work
      used to work, now broken?     ──→  a bugfix
      small and mechanical?         ──→  a quick-fix
      a standard to define?         ──→  cross-cutting
      one coherent thing?           ──→  a feature
      several things in one name?   ──→  an epic
                          ↓
              ◆ Have I read this right?
```
